The most important factor for high-volume applications is cost. Let's look at the raw pricing:

At face value, GPT-4o is 20% cheaper on input tokens and 33% cheaper on output tokens. However, Claude 3.5 Sonnet offers a significantly larger context window (200K vs 128K), which can matter for long-document applications and reduce the need for chunking strategies.

Speed & Latency

For real-time applications, speed often matters more than raw benchmark scores. In general testing, GPT-4o generates approximately 80–100 tokens per second, while Claude 3.5 Sonnet is similar at 75–90 tokens per second. Both are production-grade for most real-time applications.

For latency-critical applications (sub-500ms time to first token), the smaller models in each family — GPT-4o Mini and Claude 3 Haiku — are significantly more appropriate.

Coding Ability

Claude 3.5 Sonnet has a clear edge in coding tasks. In the SWE-Bench (a real-world software engineering benchmark), Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieves 49% on the verified subset — a substantial lead. If your primary use case is code generation, review, or debugging, Claude 3.5 Sonnet is the stronger choice despite the higher cost.

However, GPT-4o integrates more seamlessly with the broader OpenAI ecosystem (function calling format, Assistants API, structured outputs), which may matter if you're building within that platform.

Vision & Multimodal

Both models support image inputs. GPT-4o processes images at a fixed cost per image tile (approximately $0.002–0.01 per image depending on resolution). Claude 3.5 Sonnet's vision pricing follows the same token-based model — images are converted to tokens based on their dimensions. For high-volume image processing, benchmark both against your specific image types.

Context Window: Why 200K Matters

Claude's 200K context window compared to GPT-4o's 128K is more significant than it might appear. At 128K tokens, you can process roughly 100,000 words — about a full novel. At 200K, that becomes 150,000 words.

For practical applications this matters in:

  • Legal document review (contracts, filings)
  • Long codebase analysis (entire repositories)
  • Research paper synthesis (multiple papers at once)
  • Customer support with long conversation history

Recommendation by Use Case

Choose GPT-4o when:
  • Cost is the primary concern (20-33% cheaper)
  • You're building within the OpenAI ecosystem
  • You need tight integration with OpenAI tools (Assistants, Files API)
  • Context window >128K is not required
Choose Claude 3.5 Sonnet when:
  • Coding quality is paramount (SWE-Bench leader)
  • You process long documents (>100K tokens)
  • Math and reasoning accuracy is critical
  • You prefer Anthropic's safety-focused approach

Conclusion

Both GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet are world-class models. GPT-4o wins on price, while Claude 3.5 Sonnet wins on coding ability, math reasoning, and context window. For most general-purpose applications, the choice often comes down to ecosystem preference and specific task requirements.

For cost-sensitive, high-volume applications, GPT-4o (or even GPT-4o Mini) is the smarter economic choice. For applications where quality on complex tasks is non-negotiable, Claude 3.5 Sonnet's marginal performance advantage often justifies the price premium.
